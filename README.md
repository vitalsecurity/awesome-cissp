# Awesome CISSP

**A last-mile CISSP guide for security architects who already know the technology and need to learn to think like the business.**

You can design a zero-trust network, pick the right cipher suite, and explain Kerberos on a whiteboard. That usually isn't why strong technical people fail the CISSP. They fail because the exam keeps asking what a *manager* should do, and the best answer is almost never "fix it."

This guide covers that gap: governance, risk, accountability, law, and the managerial mindset that sits on top of your technical knowledge.

## The Approach

This guide borrows the style of the *Eleventh Hour CISSP* study guide: short, dense, and meant for final review instead of first-time learning.

- **Straight to the point.** Tables and bullets, not long paragraphs.
- **Exam callouts.** `★ Key Tip`, `★ Exam Tip`, and `✕ Not a Valid Response` boxes point out the traps.
- **Business first.** Every section frames security as a way to meet business goals, not as a technical control.
- **Memorize this.** Formulas, mnemonics, and ordered lists you can write on your scratch paper as soon as the exam starts.

## How to Use It

1. **Early in your prep:** read it once to see which business topics you're weakest on.
2. **During your prep:** use the tables to check what you learned from your main study materials.
3. **In the last 48 hours:** reread it and practice rebuilding the formulas and mnemonics from memory.
4. **At the testing center:** write the risk formulas down as soon as you accept the NDA.
